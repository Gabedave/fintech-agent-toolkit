# Acceptance and evidence

Fix the observable outcome, invariants, exclusions and expected evidence before production edits. Use user requirements, authoritative contracts and an acceptance owner independent of implementation. The implementing agent may propose a clarification; material changes to approved outcomes require the owner's decision before dependent work. Preserve the approved version or digest outside the candidate branch. Candidate code, tests or policy cannot redefine their own success.

For a policy or required-check change, retain the trusted-base requirements and conservatively combine candidate additions: union required checks/features/invariants and use the higher risk/review requirement. Unknown schema or unavailable authority is incomplete evidence. A known failed criterion or reproduced blocker remains failed even when another check is unavailable. This rule is reasoning guidance; it does not establish an active policy engine or repository ruleset.

## Pre-edit chronology

Bind risk classification, fixed expectations, applicable semantic reproductions or sensitive invariant controls, source identity and command outcomes before the first production write. Use trustworthy tool or harness events when available; otherwise state the chronology limit. Self-reported completion fields alone are insufficient evidence of execution. A baseline failure must concern missing/incorrect behavior, not an unavailable service. A behavior-preserving refactor can use passing controls that demonstrably detect a controlled unsafe variant.

When a gate was missed, record that historical finding and its disposition. Later tests may establish final behavior; they cannot turn the earlier sequence into compliant work. Preserve failures and evidence rather than reconstructing a pre-edit record after the fact.

## CI inclusion and aggregate readiness

When adding or moving required tests, inspect the actual CI selector/shard configuration and compare its collected test identities with the required tests. A local test run or mapped filename does not prove CI executed it. Use real isolated storage/runtime collaborators when that boundary carries the risk; a success stub cannot prove storage behavior. Product verifiers own commands, selectors, fixtures and safe environments.

Confirm the repository's required aggregate gate at the exact current PR head with every expected input. Individual green shards, a superseded head, or failing, pending, skipped or unavailable required evidence cannot establish readiness. A missing gate or absent product integration is an explicit gap. Toolkit scenarios and fake hosts prove only their bounded workflow behavior, not activation in a product repository.

## Evidence destination

Put review-only reports, command results and privacy-safe captures in the authorized PR description/attachments and retain originals in local temporary or ignored storage through hosted verification. Maintained product documentation, runbooks, ADRs and intentional assets belong in normal source locations. Honor an explicit user storage exception within its exact scope. Keep private working notes and raw transcripts local; publishing code does not implicitly publish those artifacts.

## Safe observability

Establish organization-specific read authorization and query scope before provider access. Prefer a purpose-built read-only interface and privacy-safe aggregates, release identities and existence checks. Distinguish missing tools, reauthentication, permission denial, unavailable configuration, timeout and query failure; try a safe authorized alternative when useful. In unattended tasks, keep interactive login blocked until a human can act. A borrowed policy or topology example grants no access. Keep credentials, customer/provider identifiers and raw logs out of queries sent externally, reports and reusable fixtures.
